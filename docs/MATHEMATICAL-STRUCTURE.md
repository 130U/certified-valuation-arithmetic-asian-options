# Mathematical structure

The [paper](../manuscript/report.md) studies one quantity, the cost of exercising an arithmetic average-price option on a scaled geometric average, and follows it from a pathwise identity to a proved price interval. The argument has seven steps.

1. **Exercise on a proxy.** For positive $`A`$, $`B`$ and a strike $`K`$, the call payoff exceeds its proxy-exercised version by $`|A-K|`$ on the event that the strike separates $`A`$ from $`B`$, and by nothing otherwise. Proposition 3.1 integrates this across strikes: against any convex $`\varphi`$ the total is the Bregman divergence $`E[\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)-\varphi'(B)(A-B)]`$. Two cases are used, $`E(A-B)^2/2`$ and $`E\,d(A/B)`$ with $`d(x)=x-1-\log x`$. No model is needed.
2. **Find the factor every fixing shares.** If the return before the first fixing has a Gaussian part with volatility $`\sigma`$ given everything else, each fixing is that lognormal factor times a known shape. Proposition 3.2 evaluates the conditional price as a Black–Scholes value and the conditional cost as a gamma integrated over the basis, or equivalently as the mass $`d(a/b)`$ spread by a Gaussian of width $`\sigma`$ in the log-strike.
3. **Bound the peak.** A Gaussian density is at most $`1/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})`$. Theorem 3.3 multiplies each total mass by that peak, which gives the two forms of the kink bound, and proves a matching lower bound. Proposition 3.4 finds the best scale, the mean of $`A/G`$ weighted by $`1/\sigma`$, where the bound is a mean log deviation. Corollary 3.5 transfers the bound to the lower bounds of Curran, Rogers–Shi and Fusai–Kyriakou.
4. **Measure it.** In Black's model everything is a Gaussian moment. Proposition 4.1 gives the bound in closed form and its leading term $`v^3\tau^2/(360\sqrt{2\pi t_1})`$ per unit of strike; Appendix I computes the constants from the spectrum of a centred random walk. Ball arithmetic turns the closed forms into proved price intervals for market-style contracts.
5. **Certify a hard case.** Under Heston the proxy value is a pair of distribution functions, inverted from 13 exponential-affine transforms, and the bound is a weighted second moment built from 91. The simulated scheme is not affine: Appendix B bounds the effect of its positive part. Appendix C controls the inversion and its infinite tails, Appendix A the quadrature of the moment. Theorem 5.1 combines them, and Corollary 5.2 states the intervals at the specified point.
6. **Explain the discretization error.** Proposition 6.1 shows that one step of the scheme is one explicit Euler step of the Riccati system, up to the positive part. The finite sums inside the certificate then exhibit a first-order bias in the proxy value. Theorem 6.2 computes the leading error on the deterministic-variance face, where it is a sensitivity to variance times the quadrature error of the Euler variance. Appendix H compares the two costs of the kink under a coupling: keeping their cancellation narrows the allowance by a factor of 175 on the deterministic-variance face.
7. **Carry the bounds through a calibration.** Theorem 7.1 bounds the displacement of every posterior quantile of the Asian price by the fixed-parameter gap plus a transport term for the fitted parameter.

The first three steps are general. Step 1 needs no model, and steps 2 and 3 hold wherever the return before the first fixing has a Gaussian part that every fixing shares: Black's model, Heston, 3/2, Stein–Stein and rough volatility, the same with jumps, and time-stepping schemes with Gaussian price innovations. Theorem 5.1, Proposition 6.1, Theorem 6.2 and Theorem 7.1 are stated for general contracts and parameters; the computations of steps 5 to 7 evaluate them at the points given in the paper.
